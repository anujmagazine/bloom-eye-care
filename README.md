# Bloom — Health Routines

A personal, fully offline health-exercise app: guided routines, reminders, and streaks, one program per health
goal. Installable as a real app on Windows/Mac and Android. No internet, no account, no data leaves the device.

Live: https://anujmagazine.github.io/bloom-eye-care/

## Programs

### 1. Dry Eye Care — one short break every working hour

| Exercise | Targets | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Blink Reset** — 10 × close (2s) → squeeze (2s) → open (2s) | Tear film, incomplete blinks | Blink-training trials, *Contact Lens & Anterior Eye* (2020, 2025) |
| **Far Gaze 20·20** — 3 × 20s looking 20 ft away | Focusing strain | 20-20-20 rule (AOA / AAO) |
| **Near–Far Focus** — 10 switches, thumb ↔ distant object | Accommodative fatigue | Standard optometric drill |
| **Smooth Convergence** — 6 slow thumb push-ups | Eye-teaming fatigue | Home pencil push-up studies |
| **Warm Palming** — 60s of warm darkness | Rest, oil-gland comfort | Classic rest technique |

### 2. Frozen Shoulder (left) — three ~5-minute sessions a day: morning, afternoon, evening

Chosen for the early, painful ("freezing") phase, where movement should stay inside a comfortable range.

| Exercise | Targets | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Pendulum** — arm hangs, body rocks: forward/back, side to side, small circles (20s each) | Relaxed joint movement | Core of the pain-limited approach that beat intensive stretching at 2 years (Diercks & Stevens, *J Shoulder Elbow Surg* 2004) |
| **Table Slide** — 8 × slide forward by leaning, hold 3s | Raising the arm (forward elevation) | Assisted range-of-motion, dosed to irritability (Kelley et al., *JOSPT* 2013) |
| **Stick Rotation** — 8 × push the left hand outward, elbow tucked, hold 3s | External rotation, usually the most restricted direction | Stretching matched to tissue irritability (Kelley et al., *JOSPT* 2013) |

**Comfort rule (shown and spoken before every shoulder session):** mild stretch, discomfort up to ~3/10 is fine;
never push into sharp pain; any ache should settle within 30 minutes. After each session a one-tap check records
how it felt. Two "too painful" sessions out of the last three trigger a reminder to ease off and check with a
doctor or physio. The log is on the Progress tab, so you can show it to your physiotherapist.

### 3. Habits — one quick check-in a day

Track anything, in two kinds:

- **Build** (e.g. walk 30 minutes, eye drops as prescribed): tick the days you did it. Shows a day streak.
- **Avoid** (e.g. alcohol): every day counts as clean unless you log that it happened. Shows "N days since last
  time". When adding one, you can enter the last time it happened so the count starts from there.

Tap any past day on a habit's calendar (Manage) to fix it. Progress → Habits shows each habit's streak, best run,
30-day success rate, and a 30-day strip. An evening reminder (time set in Settings) nudges you only if you haven't
checked in that day.

## Installing

Open the live site, then Settings → **Install Bloom as an app** (or the install icon in the address bar on
Chrome/Edge; ⋮ → *Install app* on Android). It gets its own window and a taskbar/home-screen icon.

## Reminders, streaks, sync

- **Eyes:** a reminder each working hour (hours set in Settings), plus a gentle popup after 2 hours with no break.
- **Shoulder:** a reminder at each of three daily times (set in Settings). A session counts toward whichever slot
  it's done in: morning before 12:00, afternoon 12:00–16:59, evening from 17:00.
- **While the app is open** reminders are reliable. **When fully closed**, an installed Bloom on Windows/Mac/Android
  can still notify via Periodic Background Sync. Chrome grants this to installed apps based on real usage, so it
  phases in over a few days.
- **Streaks** are tracked per program. **Sync** between devices: Settings → Export on one, Import on the other
  (imports merge, never overwrite).

## Medical note

Both programs support — never replace — prescribed treatment. Dry-eye exercises add to your drops and medication.
The shoulder exercises support the physiotherapy your doctor prescribed; check the range with your physio. Stop any
exercise that causes sharp pain.
